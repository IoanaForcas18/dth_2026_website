# What is open-source software?

## Disadvantages of open-source software

Open-source projects can depend heavily on their maintainers. If contributors lose interest, lack funding or have limited time, updates and support may become less frequent. This may leave tools outdated or unreliable.
Documentation and support may also be limited. Đurković et al. (2008) note that project knowledge is not always documented, making software harder for new users to understand. Students may consequently spend time installing, configuring or troubleshooting a programme instead of focusing on research.
There may also be challenges with quality control and coordination. Community testing can identify bugs, but cannot guarantee that every problem will be found. Disagreements and unclear planning can slow development or lead to competing versions (Đurković et al., 2008).
