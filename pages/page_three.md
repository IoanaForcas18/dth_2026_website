# What is open-source software?

Open-source software refers to programmes whose source code is publicly available, allowing users to study, use, modify and distribute it under the terms of its licence. Unlike proprietary software, where the code is controlled by the developing company, open-source software gives users more freedom and control (Bretthauer, 2001).

## CATMA: A project for European Studies

CATMA (Computer Assisted Text Markup and Analysis) is an open-source web application for qualitative text annotation, analysis and visualisation. It was developed by the forTEXT research group at the Technical University of Darmstadt to support systematic textual analysis (CATMA, n.d.).
For my European Studies thesis, CATMA could help investigate show the EU’s initial response to the 2026 Strait of Hormuz energy shock reconciles immediate energy-security concerns with longer-term climate objectives. I could annotate policy documents and examine how proposed measures relate to the European Green Deal, the Paris Agreement and the EU’s net-zero objectives. Categories such as energy security, fossil fuel dependence, renewable energy and climate commitments could help identify alignment, trade-offs and contradictions. Its visualisations could support comparison, but conclusions require qualitative interpretation.
Regarding the five checks, CATMA uses the GNU General Public License v3 (GPL-3.0), which permits use, modification and redistribution under its conditions. Its GitHub repository was last updated on 15 October 2026 and lists 13 contributors. It has 174 open issues, although developers have responded to reported problems. Its development and maintenance are funded by the German Research Foundation (DFG) (forTEXT, n.d.).
Overall, I would rely on CATMA for my thesis because it offers the qualitative analysis and visualisation features needed to compare EU policy documents and investigate potential contradictions between energy security and climate objectives. However, I would not depend on it as the only place where my research is stored. Since it is open source and allows data export, I could preserve my annotations and research material and move to another tool if CATMA no longer met my needs or became unavailable.
